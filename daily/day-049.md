# Day 049: A version number for the whole tree

**Sun 2026-08-09 · Reflect · Day 49 of 49**

Daily projects mostly end the same way: the entries thin out, the gaps grow, and after a while the
silence is the announcement. This one ends on a chosen day with paperwork instead. A last log, a
terminal line on the front page, a tag, and a release. None of that is ceremony. It is the same move
every control in this repo makes, writing a state down so a reader can tell what they are holding.

## Shipped

[log/week-07.md](../log/week-07.md), the last weekly log, which does something no earlier one could:
it reads the week against a list published in advance. [The week 6 log](../log/week-06.md) named five
deliverables on the record so this week could be checked against them, and all five landed, one of
them carrying four disclosed defects, which under
[the maintenance policy](../governance/maintenance-policy.md), the ruling on what this repo owes after
today, is what landing honestly looks like.

[The front page](../README.md) now opens on the closed fact: one artifact a day for 49 days,
2026-06-22 to 2026-08-09, complete and maintained. And with this entry committed, the tree takes the
tag v1.0.0 and a release points anyone arriving at [the field guide](../FIELD-GUIDE.md), the one page
that fits in ten minutes.

## What I learned

Thirty-four files in this repo carry their own version numbers, and for seven weeks those numbers did
the work: a bump meant a correction, a footer meant open questions, and any single file could tell you
exactly how far it had moved. What no file could tell you was the state of the whole. The pieces
version themselves; nothing versioned the set.

The tag is that missing number. v1.0.0 is the only version here that covers every file at once, and
what it buys is a sentence nobody could write before today: at v1.0.0, this was true. Every claim, every
count, every disclosed defect is now pinned to one name a reader can check out, diff against, or hold
the repo to. The census shipped its counts with commands; the tag is what keeps the commands honest,
because it freezes the tree they run against.

That is also why the release matters more than it looks. A tag is for people who already cloned the
repo. A release is the tag made visible, the one place GitHub announces that a state was declared
rather than drifted into. The difference between a finished repo and an abandoned one is that somebody
wrote down which it is, and today that writing-down became mechanical.

## After

The daily cadence is over and does not resume. The check runs weekly whether or not anyone touches the
tree, a red run gets fixed, a factual error gets corrected and bumps its file's version, and every
next-pass line in every footer stays what the policy declared it to be, named open work with nobody
assigned. Three things would reopen this, and the policy states all of them; the first is somebody
running the loop with a real team and reporting what broke, which is the one piece of evidence
forty-nine days of one person writing could not produce. Anything genuinely new starts somewhere else,
under its own name, with its own first entry.
