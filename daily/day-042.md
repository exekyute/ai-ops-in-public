# Day 042: Naming the last day

**Sun 2026-08-02 · Reflect · Day 42 of 49**

A daily project ends one of two ways. Somebody picks a date and stops on it, or the entries thin out
until the gaps between them become the answer. The second way is far more common and nobody ever
announces it. This one now has a date: Day 049, next Sunday, and then it stops.

## Shipped

[log/week-06.md](../log/week-06.md), closing the week that put four AI-specific pieces into a review
system designed before any of them existed. It carries three lessons, that a rule and its enforcement
are different objects and only one of them binds, that a check which cannot be answered from the thing
being checked needs the decision moved rather than the wording sharpened, and that every claim made this
week got narrower under checking while the narrow versions came out better.

It also does two things a weekly log here has not done before. It explains why the entries changed shape
mid-week, which is that I read this repo the way somebody arriving at it would, one file cold, and it did
not work: no document linked to another, five weekly maps described a loop none of them drew, and the
front page opened onto a folder of filenames in date order. And it states where this ends and what
finished will mean, which is complete and maintained rather than abandoned, with the CI left on and a
maintenance policy still to come.

## What I learned

Writing the ending forced a distinction the repo had been getting by without. I had been carrying
"finished" as one idea, and it is two. The design is finished: the loop has a bar, a gate, a record, four
metrics, a guardrail on the reviewer, a placement for model steps, a change procedure that reaches a
prompt, an eval that gates a change, and a check that runs on every push. The evidence is not finished at
all. Nothing here has run with a second person, a real queue, or a live model step.

Those two states want opposite responses, which is why separating them mattered. An unfinished design says
keep writing. Unfinished evidence says stop writing, because no further document by one person produces a
second reviewer. That is what makes the date defensible instead of arbitrary. Seven weeks is not a target
I hit. It is where the work stopped being writable.

The uncomfortable part went in too. The same log that claims the rewrite worked also records that nobody
has read the new entries cold, and that the person judging whether this repo is legible is the person who
could not see it was illegible for thirty-six days. Both are true and they sit three paragraphs apart on
purpose.
