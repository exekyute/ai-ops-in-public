# Day 048: One page for the reader who will not read

**Sat 2026-08-08 · Integrate · Day 48 of 49**

Ninety-one thousand words sit in this repo across eighty-nine files. Every entrance built so far assumes
somebody willing to work: an ordered index of twenty-four pieces, a front page that routes to five
places, weekly maps that each take a week at a time. None of that helps the reader who arrives with ten
minutes and no reason to trust any of it.

## Shipped

[FIELD-GUIDE.md](../FIELD-GUIDE.md) at the repo root, 1,160 words. What the system is, the loop in five
sentences and then drawn, the ten files worth opening, what is real and what is authored, what broke and
what caught it, how to adopt it, and what it does not prove. [The front page](../README.md) now leads
with it, so the first link a visitor sees is the shortest honest path through everything else.

## What I learned

I set a lens on this whose only job was to read the draft with no access to the repo and report the
exact sentence where it wanted to stop. That is a cheap thing to arrange and it found things no amount
of re-reading by me would have. It flagged a workflow described as "one n8n workflow" with no clue what
n8n is, a limit stated five separate times, and a topic sentence saying the standards had been "used,"
which quietly implies people ran them on real work when nobody has.

That last one is the useful catch, because it was not a false claim so much as a word doing more work
than it had earned. A reader deciding whether to trust this repo hits "used" and reads operational
experience into it, then two paragraphs later hits "nothing here has run with a real team" and has to
decide which sentence is the honest one. Every fix in this file was of that shape: not correcting a
falsehood, but closing the distance between what a sentence says and what it will be taken to mean.

The other change came from asking whether the evidence section was as strong as it sounded. It lists
three things that broke and were caught. On inspection, one was caught by a control. The other two were
caught by a person who happened to be writing about something else at the time. That is a weaker claim
about the controls and a much more interesting one, so the guide now says it in those words, and follows
it with the line that matters: one found error implies an unknown number of unfound ones.
