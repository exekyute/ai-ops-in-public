# Day 046: Seventeen days of saying four

**Thu 2026-08-06 · Document · Day 46 of 49**

Something over eighty thousand words arrived here in seven weeks at one artifact a day. Anybody reading
carefully has already worked out that a model drafted most of them. Today says so plainly, describes the
process, and shows the record of that process catching its own mistakes. Writing it turned up one it had
missed for two and a half weeks.

## Shipped

[docs/how-this-repo-is-made.md](../docs/how-this-repo-is-made.md), in a new folder, linked from the
front page. It sets out how a day gets made: the subject comes from what the previous day left open, I
write the brief including the argument I think is right, two drafts get generated and merged, several
adversarial passes attack the result with one job each, and then I read everything and decide.

The part that matters is the evidence, because a claim that something gets checked is worth nothing
unless the checking has caught things. Three catches are cited, each pointing at a dated entry anybody
can open, and each one narrowed or overturned an argument I had brought to the day.

It also names the two limits that matter. The drafting and the checking run on the same system, from the
same brief, prompted by the same person, which is the trust-relocation problem
[the spot-audit guardrail](../governance/review-spot-audit.md), the control that re-reviews the reviewer,
already describes. And I originate the brief and approve the result, which by this repo's own audit rule
is a disqualified position.

Alongside it, [the review log spec](../sops/review-log-spec.md) went to v4.

## What I learned

I had a line ready about how you cannot count what a process misses, because a catch is visible and a
miss is not. It was true and it was cheap, the kind of caveat that costs nothing to write. Then the
fact-checking pass went looking for a live example of exactly that, and found one in the file the review
side reads more than any other.

The log spec has said "four required fields" since its first version, when four was correct. A later
version added a fifth, updated the field list, and left that sentence alone. It has been wrong since,
through two version bumps and every pass that has read that file, which by now is many. Seventeen days.

Two things about that are worth keeping. The first is that nothing in this repo could have caught it.
The index check reads paths, titles, versions and one tally on one page; a file quietly disagreeing with
itself in prose is precisely the meaning-level drift its own notes say a person has to find, and no
person looked at that line for two and a half weeks. The second is that the caveat stopped being cheap
the moment it had an example attached. "You cannot count the misses" is a sentence anybody can write.
"Here is one, it lasted seventeen days, and here is why nothing caught it" is a different claim, and it
cost something to make.

The fix took a minute and followed the rule shipped two days ago: correct the error, bump the version.
The version bump then broke the index, which cited the old number in two places, and the check caught
that on the next run. That part worked exactly as designed.
